TI ASYMPTOTIC EXPANSION OF SMOOTH FUNCTIONS IN DETERMINISTIC AND IID HAAR
   UNITARY MATRICES, AND APPLICATION TO TENSOR PRODUCTS OF MATRICES
SO ANNALS OF PROBABILITY
LA English
DT Article
DE Random Matrix theory; Free Probability theory; Asymptotic expansion
ID MODELS; LIMIT
AB Let U-N be a family of N & times; N independent Haar unitary random matrices and their adjoints, Z(N) a family of deterministic matrices, and P a self-adjoint noncommutative polynomial, that is, for any N, P(U-N, Z(N)) is self-adjoint, f a smooth function. We prove that for any k, if f is smooth enough, there exist deterministic constants alpha(P)(i)(f,Z(N))such that E[1/N Tr(f(P(U-N, Z(N))))] = Sigma(i=0) (k) alpha(i) (P) (f, Z(N)) / N-2i +O(N-2k-2). Besides, the constants alpha(P)(i)(f, Z(N))are built explicitly with the help of free probability. As a corollary, we prove that given alpha < 1/2, for N large enough, every eigenvalue of P(U-N, Z(N)) is N-alpha-close to the spectrum of P(u, Z(N)) where u is a d-tuple of free Haar unitaries. We also prove the convergence of the norm of any polynomial P(U-N circle times I-M,I-N circle times Y-M) as long as the family Y-M converges strongly and that M << N ln(-3)(N).

TI REFLECTING POISSON WALKS AND DYNAMICAL UNIVERSALITY IN p-ADIC RANDOM
   MATRIX THEORY

LA English
DT Article
DE p-adic random matrices; cokernels; Poisson random walks; interacting
   particle systems
ID STOCHASTIC DIFFERENTIAL-EQUATIONS; EIGENVALUES; PRODUCTS; TILINGS; PATHS
AB We prove dynamical local limits for the singular numbers of p-adic random matrix products at both the bulk and edge. The limit object which we construct, the reflecting Poisson sea, may thus be viewed as a p-adic analogue of line ensembles appearing in classical random matrix theory. However, in contrast to those it is a discrete space Poisson-type particle system with only local reflection interactions and no obvious determinantal structure. The limits hold for any GLn(& Zopf;p)-invariant matrix distributions under weak universality hypotheses, with no spatial rescaling.

AB Our bodies mediate every interaction with technology, yet-as soma design and feminist HCI remind us-the body is not a neutral canvas. We introduce and examine felt asymmetries-somaesthetic experiences of difference in the body-as a site for generative and critical engagement in interaction design. Through an autobiographical design exploration, and a series of somatic explorations with nine designers including individual inquiries and workshops, we sensitised to, articulated, and shared personal experiences of asymmetry. DE Error Analysis; Post-hoc Correction; Trustworthy AI
AB The widespread adoption of machine learning in critical applications demands techniques to mitigate high-consequence errors. Our method utilizes a dual-classifier GBDT pipeline to distinguish routine human-like errors from high-risk non-human misclassifications. Evaluated across three domains, animal breed classification, skin lesion diagnosis (ISIC 2018), and prostate histopathology (SICAPv2), our framework demonstrates robust safety improvements. To address real-world deployment concerns, our results confirm the pipeline introduces negligible inference latency (1.60% overhead for the animal dataset, 1.84% for ISIC, and 1.70% for SICAPv2) while outperforming traditional Maximum Class Probability (MCP) baselines in correction precision. This proves that safety-critical reliability can be substantially enhanced post-hoc without expensive model retraining.

AB This study introduces a hybrid dome structure that combines a dielectric lens (DL) and a metadome (MD) to enhance the scanning capabilities of phased array antennas (PAAs). These configurations show great potential for improving the scanning capacity of PAAs with a lightweight dome while allowing a broad frequency range. Since traditional full-wave simulations of such electrically large structures are computationally intensive and time-consuming, the radiation characteristics of hybrid dome structures are analysed using a simplified two-dimensional (2-D) ray-tracing and physical-optics (RT-PO) model. AB The 'Footsteps Illusion' shows that pitch-altering footstep sounds in real-time affects body perception, gait, and emotion: high versus low frequencies evoke a lighter versus heavier body. We tested whether this illusion extends beyond the lab, where environmental factors matter. Using a mixed-methods approach, twenty-eight participants used a minimal setup to synchronize with three prerecorded pitch-altered footstep soundtracks: Control, High-Frequency, and Low-Frequency. In Experiment 1, they walked a fixed path, with gait recordings, questionnaires, and body visualizations. In Experiment 2, they walked freely across campus routes with High/Low-Frequency soundtracks, followed by post-walk interviews using a novel spatial mapping tool linking body sensations to context. Results replicate the illusion outdoors, showing synchronization with prerecorded sounds as a viable alternative to real-time feedback, and revealing that contextual factors modulate illusion effects. We contribute insights and novel tools (prototype, spatial mapping method, and footstep soundbase) for studying multisensory body perception in everyday contexts.

TI A tax-subsidy scheme for efficient investment in renewable generation
   capacity
SO ELECTRIC POWER SYSTEMS RESEARCH
LA English
DT Article
DE Renewable energy; Electricity generation capacity; Market power;
   Environmental externalities; Incentives
ID ENERGY; BEHAVIOR
AB Electricity markets are central to the energy transition, but current market rules do not always deliver socially desirable outcomes. This paper studies two related inefficiencies in wholesale electricity markets. First, competitive market-clearing based on merit-order dispatch can ignore pollution damages, leading to inefficient spot-market outcomes. We address this distortion by deriving a Pigouvian tax that charges each generator for its marginal contribution to pollution damages. Second, because generation capacity affects future spot prices, producers may strategically distort their long-run investment decisions, particularly in systems with increasingly low-operating-cost renewable generation. To align private investment incentives with social welfare, we derive a generator-specific investment subsidy based on each producer's contribution to aggregate utility. We show that, under the stated behavioral and informational assumptions, the combined tax-subsidy mechanism aligns decentralized dispatch and generation-capacity investment decisions with the social-welfare benchmark while respecting operational constraints. Implementing this mechanism does not require producers to report their complete private cost functions; the tax and subsidy can instead be calculated using existing market submissions and settlement data, verified emission information, and generator-specific counterfactual market re-clearing outcome. An analytical example and numerical experiments illustrate the mechanism and show that strategic capacity investment can exceed the socially optimal level and reduce welfare.

AB During the flow-induced compaction of saturated deformable fibrous media, local nonuniformity in solid volume fraction can develop, persist after compression, and partially relax through elastic rebound. Using an experimental approach that spatially resolves the local volume fraction in situ, we characterize the formation and elastic relaxation of this nonuniformity, which manifests as a solid-fraction gradient, in two distinct lignocellulosic fibre networks and over a range of compression rates and bulk solid fractions. The behavioural intention to choose the path did not differ between head torches, but the intention to avoid the path was lower with OHT. No differences in comfort aspects of the light could be identified. As expected, the urban forest was rated higher for the environmental appraisals in DL, but retained its restorative potential even in the two head torch conditions. It is concluded that light sources with less ecological impact could be introduced without influencing pedestrians' environmental experiences negatively.
